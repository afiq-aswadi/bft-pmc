What does a Bayes-filtered transformer believe?

A Bayes-filtered transformer is trained to predict observations generated from a latent task . In the idealized limit, its predictions reproduce the Bayesian posterior predictive distribution. What prior and posterior does a trained model approximate in practice?

Predictive Monte Carlo (PMC) addresses this question by generating sequences from the model and estimating the latent task from each completed sequence. Explore the resulting distributions for linear regression, balls-and-urns, and Markov chains, and compare them with the memorizing and generalizing references studied in the paper.

04 — About

How to interpret the distributions

PMC

PMC estimate

PMC generates multiple continuations from the same prompt and estimates the latent task from each completed sequence. The distribution of these estimates approximates the model’s implicit prior when the prompt is empty, or its implicit posterior when observations are supplied.

Memorizing reference

The memorizing reference uses the empirical prior over the training tasks. Before observing a prompt , those tasks have equal probability. After conditioning, their probabilities are updated using the prompt likelihood. The posterior remains supported on the training tasks and may favor one or several.

Generalizing reference

The generalizing reference uses the population prior from which the training tasks were sampled: Gaussian over regression coefficients , Dirichlet over urn proportions , and independently Dirichlet over each row of a Markov transition matrix . Individual probability coordinates in the categorical families have Beta marginals.

Reading a panel

Density plots show where samples concentrate. CDFs show how probability accumulates. The memorizing reference is discrete, so its exact CDF is a weighted step function. It need not consist of a single jump.

The explorer represents both references with finite samples. Histogram widths and smoothing affect density peaks. Interpret a narrow peak through its probability mass rather than its height alone. Tall reference bars may be clipped.

Coordinate ranges can differ between panels. Read the axis labels when comparing locations or spreads. The dotted blue curve is the analytic generalizing marginal.

Rollout length

Increasing rollout length changes the finite-sequence estimate of the latent task . It can reduce some estimation effects, but does not guarantee monotonically improving accuracy or agreement with either reference. Short rollouts should not generally be interpreted as prior samples.

For linear regression, counts covariate–response pairs. For balls-and-urns and Markov chains, it counts observations, excluding the beginning-of-sequence token. The explorer includes rollouts beyond the training context. Linear regression and balls-and-urns use no positional encoding. The extended Markov runs use rescaled rotary positions.

Data and reproducibility

The explorer displays 512 samples per distribution from saved diagnostic runs. Each posterior configuration uses one in-distribution prompt of length . The paper’s posterior sweeps instead average distances over multiple evaluation prompts.

The paper and supplementary material describe the experimental settings, reference distributions, and reliability analyses. Code and data provenance are available in the project repository.

Interpreting PMC estimates as implicit priors and posteriors relies on conditions discussed in the paper that are not established for the trained models. Stable estimates alone do not establish accurate recovery. Disagreement with a reference can reflect model–reference mismatch, finite-rollout estimation effects, and sampling variability.
